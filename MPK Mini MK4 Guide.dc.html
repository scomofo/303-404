This is a production course, not a DJ course

The MK4 is a compact MIDI keyboard and pad controller for making music — beats, synth parts, DAW control — not a DJ controller. It also makes no sound on its own: it sends MIDI, and software makes the noise. So a real chunk of "learning the MK4" is learning MPC Beats or your DAW, and the weeks below are built that way.

Every practice widget in this course is synthesized in the browser — drums, keys, chords, arpeggios and all — so you can rehearse the mechanics before your own software is set up. Nothing here sends real MIDI; it stands in for the hardware while you learn what each control does.

Velocity is how hard you hit the pad. Everything at full velocity is the single most common giveaway of a beginner's beat — hit the same pad at Soft, then Full, and listen to the difference. With Note Repeat armed, a tap fires a run of hits at the chosen rate instead of one, which is how rolls get played.

Held chord
{{ ctl.name }}
{{ arpSequenceLabel }}

Freeze locks the note order that is running now, so changing the held chord leaves the shape alone. Mutate nudges one step of that order every time the pattern comes round — always to another note of the same chord, so it wanders without going wrong. Both are simplified stand-ins for the hardware's own arp; use them to learn what to listen for, then find the same controls on the unit.

Two tracks down.

You wired the MK4 up, learned the panel, programmed beats on the pads with real velocity, played in key with Scale and Chord Mode, put the arpeggiator and the knobs to work, mapped the controller to your DAW, and arranged two finished pieces in different styles. From here the controller is no longer the thing holding you back.

Where to go next

  • A deeper course in your DAW — Ableton, FL Studio, Logic or MPC Beats. The MK4 is solved; growth now comes from software and production skill.
  • Basic mixing: EQ, compression and level balance, to make finished tracks sound fuller.
  • Music theory basics, if you want to work past the guardrails Scale and Chord Mode give you.
  • Melodics — or padlab, your own pad trainer — for continued finger-drumming technique.
